# How to Read and Understand Your Electricity Bill: An EKZ Bill, Line by Line

The electricity bill is one of those documents people pay without reading. Yet its structure is surprisingly transparent: Every item has a clear purpose, a clear issuer, and a clear answer to the question of whether you can change it. Once you understand the four sections, you can read any Swiss electricity bill, because the structure is prescribed by law and is the same for all grid operators.

This article walks through an invoice from EKZ (Elektrizitätswerke des Kantons Zürich), our grid operator, item by item. The interactive sample bill below follows the structure of our own quarterly bill; the figures represent a model household with consumption of 1,800 kWh per quarter, calculated using actual 2026 EKZ rates.

## Who is actually involved in the bill

An electricity bill has three issuers, even though only one sends it:

1. **The energy supplier** sells the kilowatt-hours. Under the basic supply service, this is the local utility; prices are reviewed by the Price Supervisor and the ElCom, respectively. This is the only section where you can choose a product.
2. **The grid operator** transports the electricity. The grid is a regulated monopoly: You cannot switch it, and the ElCom reviews the rates. There are, however, optional rates here and, since 2026, the LEG discount.
3. **The federal government, canton, and municipality** add fees: grid surcharge, electricity reserve, municipal charges. Neither the supplier nor the grid operator can change these.

## The four sections in detail

For everyone who prefers to read continuous text (and for search engines), here are the same items in detail.

### Energy supply: the only section with product choice

Energy supply is the electricity itself. In the basic supply service, where the vast majority of households are, the local utility sets the rate annually and the ElCom reviews it. At EKZ, the standard product is called “EKZ Energie Erneuerbar” and costs 13.30 Rp./kWh in the winter half-year and 9.00 Rp./kWh in the summer half-year in 2026 (excl. VAT).

What disappeared in 2026 is notable: peak and off-peak rates. Instead of “expensive during the day, cheap at night,” there is now a single rate that changes by quarter. The traditional advice to run the washing machine at night is therefore outdated from a rate perspective; the season matters, not the time of day. Those who want more precision can switch to the dynamic optional rate, in which the price follows the market price hourly.

There is also a fixed base rate of CHF 3.00 per month.

### Grid usage: the regulated monopoly

Grid usage pays for the construction, operation, and maintenance of lines, transformer stations, and substations. You cannot switch grid operators; in return, rates are regulated and subject to ElCom review.

Within this monopoly, however, there are options that may be worthwhile. EKZ offers three grid products for households in 2026:

| Grid product | Energy rate (excl. VAT) | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EKZ Netz 400ST (standard) | 7.95 Rp./kWh | none |
| EKZ Netz 400F | 7.50 Rp./kWh | EKZ may control flexible loads (water heater, heat pump) in a grid-supportive way |
| EKZ Netz 400WP | 6.45 Rp./kWh | Heating applications with control |

Anyone who allows their water heater to be controlled thus saves just under half a centime per kilowatt-hour compared with the standard rate. And since 2026, there has been a second lever on this item: Anyone who joins a local electricity community (LEG) receives a statutory discount of 20 or 40 percent on the grid-usage energy rate for electricity traded locally. What an LEG is is covered in [a separate article](/blog/lokale-elektrizitaetsgemeinschaft-leg-erklaert); whether it pays off is covered [in the next one](/blog/lohnt-sich-leg-beitritt).

The “system services” item (0.27 Rp./kWh) goes to Swissgrid for the stability of the overall system: frequency control, balancing energy, black-start capability.

### Metering: now visible

Since 2026, EKZ has listed metering costs separately: CHF 5.00 per month for the meter, data transmission, and provision of metering data. Previously, this was invisibly included in grid usage. The smart meter paid for here measures in 15-minute intervals and is the technical basis for everything the electricity market is currently learning: dynamic rates, LEG billing, and load shifting.

### Surcharges and fees: the government section

Four items over which neither the supplier nor the grid operator has any influence:

- **Federal fees** (2.30 Rp./kWh): the grid surcharge under the Energy Act, which finances support for renewable energy and the refurbishment of hydropower.
- **Electricity reserve** (0.41 Rp./kWh): the country’s insurance premium against electricity shortages, introduced after the winter of 2022/23. It pays for the hydropower reserve and reserve power plants.
- **Shared costs** (0.05 Rp./kWh): grid reinforcements allocated across Switzerland, such as connections for solar installations.
- **Energy efficiency support** (0.16 Rp./kWh): cantonal and municipal support programs and energy consulting. Municipal concession fees may be added depending on where you live.

Finally, at the bottom is VAT: 8.1 percent on everything, including the fees.

## What remains per kilowatt-hour

If the sample bill is converted to a single kilowatt-hour (summer quarter, 400F rate), the picture is approximately this: 9.0 Rp. for energy, 7.8 Rp. for the grid and system services, 2.9 Rp. for fees, plus proportional base and metering rates and VAT. The result: Of around 21 to 22 centimes per kWh, not even half is the electricity itself. When people discuss electricity prices, half the discussion is about the grid and government.

This is precisely why it is worth looking at the individual items: A household’s biggest single lever is consumption itself, followed by the choice of grid product, energy product, and now the LEG.
